# How South African corporates buy strategy work

Research for issue #4 ("How South African corporates buy strategy work"). Researched 2026-11-22.

> **How this was researched.** Web search worked, but page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked in this session for `www.gov.za` and other hosts (DNS refused by the session's network allowlist). So **no primary document was opened and read in full.** Every claim below comes from search-result extracts of the cited pages. Where the cited page is a primary source (Government Gazette, dtic/B-BBEE Commission template, a corporate's own supplier page) the claim is marked **[primary, extract]**. Where it comes from law-firm or practitioner commentary it is marked **[secondary]**. Claims that rest on my general knowledge and were not confirmed by any source this session are marked **[UNVERIFIED]**. Before quoting any number to a client, open the gazette and check it.

## Answer

- **Large SA corporates buy strategy/design work through procurement, not just through the marketing head.** The business owner (head of marketing, CX, innovation or strategy) chooses and sponsors the work, but the supplier has to be onboarded onto the corporate's own vendor system before a PO can be raised, and bigger jobs go out as RFPs or are bought from a pre-approved panel. [Partly UNVERIFIED: no corporate publishes its internal sign-off rules. Panels and RFPs are documented for public-sector buyers, see below.]
- **Onboarding pack is fairly standard:** CIPC registration, SARS tax compliance status (PIN), bank confirmation letter, directors' IDs, company profile, and a **B-BBEE certificate or sworn affidavit**. Standard Bank and Shoprite say so on their own supplier pages. **CSD registration is required by organs of state (government and SOEs), not by private corporates.** Some corporates ask for a CSD report anyway, and SOEs and the SARB require it.
- **POPIA:** if Spotted Ape handles customer or employee personal information for the client (research participants, CRM data, interviews), the client has to sign a written **operator agreement** with it under section 21 of POPIA. Expect a data-processing addendum in the MSA. [secondary]
- **An EME affidavit gives the buyer an automatic recognition level with no verification:** **Level 4 / 100%** by default, **Level 2 / 125%** if ≥51% black-owned, **Level 1 / 135%** if 100% black-owned. Every rand spent with Spotted Ape counts at that percentage toward the buyer's Preferential Procurement score. [primary, extract: 2013 Amended Codes]
- **What makes Spotted Ape valuable to a buyer depends on its black-ownership percentage, which we don't know.** Any EME spend helps the buyer's "spend with EMEs" line (a separate target, about 15% of spend and 4 points in the generic code). But the **heaviest line (spend with ≥51% black-owned suppliers, 50% target, 11 points)** and the **30%+ black-women-owned line (12%, 4 points)** count only if Spotted Ape crosses those ownership thresholds. Under 51% black ownership, Spotted Ape is "just" a 100%-recognition EME.
- **Designated Group Supplier** (≥51% owned by black youth, black people with disabilities, unemployed black people, black people in rural areas, or black military veterans) earns the buyer **bonus points**. Spotted Ape can only be one if its ownership matches.
- **ESD programmes:** big banks and retailers run Enterprise and Supplier Development programmes. Being accepted as an ESD beneficiary can bring the buyer extra scorecard points (for example, paying the supplier within 15 days counts as ESD contribution). These programmes are usually aimed at **black-owned** EMEs/QSEs, so eligibility again turns on ownership.
- **The rules may change soon.** The dtic published **draft** amendments to the Codes on 29 Jan 2026 (Gazette 54032): a Transformation Fund as an alternative to ESD, re-weighted procurement points with new lines for 100% black-owned and 100% black-women-owned suppliers. I found **no evidence that they have been finalised** as at Nov 2026.
- **Contract sizes and formats:** **no public data was found** for private-sector strategy or design engagements. Public-sector equivalents (SARB, DBSA, SABC, Presidency) buy through **multi-year panels** (typically 3 years) and then call off individual projects.

## 1. Procurement process

### 1.1 Who initiates and signs off

- **[UNVERIFIED: general practice, not from a source]** In large SA corporates the **business owner** (CMO, head of CX, innovation or strategy) identifies the need, briefs suppliers, and owns the budget. **Procurement / strategic sourcing** runs supplier onboarding, competitive quotes or RFPs above a threshold, and contracting. **Legal** reviews the MSA and the POPIA operator terms. Finance releases the PO. Delegation-of-authority thresholds decide whether a single quote, three quotes or a full RFP is needed. None of the corporates checked publish these thresholds.
- Standard Bank says it "is interested in working with all suppliers and in particular, those that meet certain criteria such as B-BBEE compliance, black ownership, black female ownership, and supplier location and industry." **[primary, extract]** https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/personal/products-and-services/ways-to-bank/supplier-marketplace

### 1.2 Supplier panels and preferred-supplier lists

- The public sector and SOEs publish their panels. The SARB is recruiting a **content creation panel** (published 2026-02-18) and a **Comms panel**. **[primary, extract]** https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/rfp/4990261-content-creation-panel ; https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/rfp/2026/Comms%20Panel.pdf
- The SARB also ran an RFP for **enterprise change management** (change capability), the nearest public analogue to strategy consulting. **[primary, title only]** https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/rfp/2025/rfp-4975215---enterprise-change-management/RFP%204975215%20-%20Tender%20Document%20Change%20Capability.pdf
- DBSA RFP090/2026 for a "Digital Agency" / integrated digital marketing partner. **[primary, title only]** https://www.dbsa.org/sites/ppdf.dbsa.org/files/media/documents/2026-05/RFP090-2026%20Digital%20Agency.pdf
- The Presidency appointed a **panel of agencies for media production and marketing services for three years**. **[primary, extract]** https://www.gov.za/documents/other/presidency-service-providers-appointed-panel-agencies-media-production-and
- Standard Bank Malawi ran an RFP for a "Strategy and Digital Agency", which shows the bank group goes to market for strategy agencies through formal RFPs. It is outside SA, so treat it only as an indication. **[primary, title only]** https://sponsorships.standardbank.com/static_file/Malawi/Downloadable%20files/Request%20for%20Proposal_Strategy%20and%20Digital%20Agency.pdf
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Private corporates (banks, insurers, telcos, retailers) also keep preferred-supplier or roster lists for agencies and consultancies, but do not publish them. Getting onto one usually comes from winning a first small project or an RFP.

Notes:
- A sworn affidavit is **valid for 12 months** from the date the Commissioner of Oaths signs it. The Commissioner **cannot be an employee** of the enterprise, and the deponent must tick **exactly one** status level. **[secondary, summarising the B-BBEE Commission's 2018 practice guide]** https://www.rsm.global/southafrica/insights/legal-insights/determining-validity-sworn-affidavit-b-bbee-compliance
- The B-BBEE Commission has said that **B-BBEE certificates issued to EMEs are invalid**, because no verification agency has authority to issue them. EMEs must use the affidavit. **[primary, extract: SAnews, government news agency]** https://www.sanews.gov.za/index.php/node/40005
- The affidavit template is free on the dtic site. Sector codes have their own templates (for example an ICT EME affidavit). Spotted Ape should check which template each client's sector asks for. Corporates often republish the templates, for example Sun International's CoGP (generic) and ICT EME templates. **[primary, extract]** https://corporate.suninternational.com/content/dam/approved/corporate/procurement/CoGP-EME-Affidavit-Template.pdf ; https://corporate.suninternational.com/content/dam/approved/corporate/procurement/ICT-EME-Affidavit-Template.pdf
- **Which template applies:** the affidavit follows the **supplier's** sector code, not the buyer's. As a design-strategy agency, Spotted Ape is most likely measured under the **generic Codes of Good Practice (CoGP) EME affidavit**. **[UNVERIFIED: confirm with a B-BBEE adviser. If Spotted Ape's main work were classed as marketing/advertising, a sector code could apply.]**

### 1.4 POPIA data agreements

- Section 21(1): the responsible party (the client) must, **through a written contract**, make sure the operator (Spotted Ape) establishes and maintains the s19 security safeguards. Section 21(2): the operator must notify the responsible party **immediately** if personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person. **[secondary]** https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2023/09/beware-of-the-operator-contract-a-necessity-for-popia-compliance
- Typical clauses: specified security measures, audit rights, no subcontracting without consent, and return or deletion of data on termination. **[secondary]** https://werksmans.com/are-your-contracts-with-suppliers-contractors-sufficient-to-ensure-compliance-with-popia/page/5/
- The Information Regulator has held a company liable for a breach at its third-party provider when there was no operator contract, so corporates will insist on one. **[secondary, same Werksmans source]** Primary: Information Regulator, Condition 7 security safeguards https://inforegulator.org.za/knowledge-base/category/popia/chapter-3-conditions-for-lawful-processing/part-a-processing-of-personal-information-in-general/condition-7-security-safeguards/

### 1.5 Contract sizes and formats

- **No public information was found** on typical rand values or project-versus-retainer splits for private-sector strategy or design work in SA. Treat any figure as unverified.
- Public-sector analogues use **multi-year panels** (3 years at the Presidency and SABC) with project call-offs. **[primary/aggregator extract, see 1.2]**
- **[UNVERIFIED]** In practice, new suppliers at big corporates usually start with a **fixed-scope project** (discovery, CX diagnostic, innovation sprint) on a PO under an MSA. Retainers come after trust is established, or through a roster RFP.

## 2. B-BBEE implications for an EME supplier

### 2.1 What an EME is and what its affidavit says

- An EME has annual total revenue of **R10m or less**. It only needs an annual **sworn affidavit** confirming (a) turnover of R10m or less and (b) its **level of black ownership**. EMEs are **not measured on a scorecard**. **[secondary, consistent with 2013 Amended Codes]** https://www.rsm.global/southafrica/news/legal-insights/b-bbee-frequently-asked-questions
- Sector codes may set different turnover thresholds and have their own affidavits. **[secondary]** same RSM source.

### 2.2 Recognition level the buyer gets (Preferential Procurement)

Under the Amended Codes of Good Practice (Gazette 36928, Notice 1019 of 11 Oct 2013, as amended 2019):

| Spotted Ape's black ownership (flow-through) | Deemed status | Procurement recognition for the buyer |
|---|---|---|
| < 51% | Level 4 | **100%** |
| ≥ 51% | Level 2 (elevated) | **125%** |
| 100% | Level 1 (elevated) | **135%** |

Sources: "an EME which is 100% Black owned qualifies for elevation to 'Level One Contributor' having a B-BBEE recognition level of 135%". EMEs are by default "Level Four Contributors" with a 100% recognition level, and ≥51% black-owned EMEs reach Level Two at 125%. **[primary, extract]** https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/36928gen1019.pdf

- Enhanced recognition counts only black ownership measured on a **direct flow-through** basis. The B-BBEE Commission's practice guide says the modified flow-through principle may not be used for it. Law firms dispute whether a guide can impose this. **[secondary]** https://www.ensafrica.com/news/detail/1424/recent-amendments-to-the-b-bbee-codes ; https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/CorporateCommercial-Law/588746/Possible-Limitations-On-The-Enhanced-B-BBEE-Recognition-For-EMEs-And-QSEs
- **What this means:** a buyer spending R1m with Spotted Ape claims R1m, R1.25m or R1.35m of "B-BBEE procurement spend from all empowering suppliers", depending on Spotted Ape's black ownership.
- **Empowering supplier test:** for other suppliers, the "empowering supplier" criteria (job creation, local manufacturing and so on) must be met for spend to count. The mechanism **applies to all supplier categories except EMEs**, so an EME counts by default. **[secondary]** https://bizcommunity.com/Article/196/610/103127.html

### 2.3 Which scorecard lines Spotted Ape helps (generic Statement 400, as amended 2019)

The Preferential Procurement sub-element of Enterprise & Supplier Development (ESD). Targets are % of the buyer's Total Measured Procurement Spend (TMPS):

| Indicator | Target / points | Does Spotted Ape count? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spend from all empowering suppliers (at recognition level) | 80% / 5 pts **[UNVERIFIED figure]** | **Yes**, at 100/125/135% | Codes; figure from memory, not confirmed this session |
| Spend from empowering **QSEs** | 15% / 3 pts | No (Spotted Ape is an EME) | [secondary] Bizcommunity below |
| Spend from **EMEs** | 15% / 4 pts | **Yes, regardless of ownership** | [primary, extract] Gazette 42496 ; [secondary] Bizcommunity |
| Spend from suppliers **≥51% black-owned** | **50% / 11 pts** (raised from 40% / 9 pts in 2019) | **Only if ≥51% black-owned** | [secondary] https://www.rsm.global/southafrica/node/415 ; https://www.bizcommunity.com/Article/196/820/191649.html |
| Spend from suppliers **≥30% black-women-owned** | 12% / 4 pts | **Only if ≥30% black-women-owned** | [primary, extract] https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201905/42496gen304.pdf |
| Bonus: spend from **Designated Group Suppliers** (≥51% owned by black youth / disabled / unemployed / rural / military veterans) | 2% / 2 bonus pts | **Only if ownership qualifies** | [primary, extract] https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201905/42496gen303.pdf ; [secondary] ENSafrica |

The total for the element is 25 points plus 2 bonus, but **that total was not confirmed** by any source retrieved this session.
Bizcommunity source for the EME and QSE split: https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/preferential-procurement-and-what-it-can-do-for-your-b-bbee-score-125336a

Also: Preferential Procurement is a **priority element** with a sub-minimum. If a large company fails it, it is **discounted one level**, which is why procurement teams chase these lines. **[UNVERIFIED in this session: well known from the 2013 Codes, but no extract was retrieved]**

### 2.4 Sector codes for target clients

- **Financial Sector Code (banks, insurers):** the amended FSC took effect **1 December 2017** and set higher procurement targets for empowering suppliers that are ≥51% black-owned and ≥30% black-owned. **[secondary]** https://webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/amended-b-bbee-financial-sector-code-effective-from-1-december-2017.aspx ; primary gazette (not opened): https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201712/41287gon1325.pdf. **The exact FSC point weights were not retrieved.** The EME recognition levels are the same as the generic codes. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **ICT Sector Code (telecoms):** the amended ICT Code was gazetted **7 Nov 2016** (Gazette 40407) and took effect on publication. **[secondary/primary title]** https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201611/40407gon1387.pdf. **The exact procurement weights were not retrieved.**
- **Retail** uses the generic codes (no retail sector code). **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Draft 2026 amendments say sector-code companies keep their current scoring until their sector code is aligned. **[secondary]** https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/BEE/Black-economic-empowerment-alert-4-February-Proposed-revisions-to-the-ESD-Scorecard-in-the-draft-B-BBEE-Code-amendments

### 2.5 Enterprise and Supplier Development (ESD)

- Besides procurement, buyers score **Supplier Development** (support to their own suppliers) and **Enterprise Development** points. Beneficiaries are typically EMEs/QSEs that are ≥51% black-owned. **[UNVERIFIED wording: the general structure is confirmed by the 2013 Codes, but the exact beneficiary definition was not retrieved]**
- **Faster payment counts:** paying an ED/SD beneficiary within 15 days of invoice lets the buyer claim (15 − days to pay)% of the invoice as an ESD contribution. Paying on day 5 earns 10%. **[primary, extract]** https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/36928gen1019.pdf
- **Standard Bank's supplier development programme** is open to **Tier 1** (active suppliers on its procurement database) and **Tier 2** (suppliers to its suppliers) entities. It asks for a B-BBEE certificate or affidavit, tax PIN, director CVs and so on. **[primary, extract]** https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/business/products-and-services/business-solutions/industry/enterprise-development-solutions-frequently-asked-questions
- **Draft change:** the Jan 2026 draft lets companies score ESD points by contributing to a **Transformation Fund** (for example 3% of NPAT for 20 points) instead. If it is finalised, corporates' in-house ESD programmes, and so the ESD route into them, may shrink. **[secondary]** https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/draft-amendments-to-b-bbee-codes-open-way-for-transformation-fund-2026-01-29 ; https://werksmans.com/unpacking-the-significant-proposed-changes-to-the-generic-codes-of-good-practice-codes-on-broad-based-black-economic-empowerment-bbbee/page/8/
- The draft also proposes **reducing** points for 51% black-owned and 30% black-women-owned spend, and **adding** lines for **100% black-owned** and **100% black-women-owned** suppliers and for 100% designated-group suppliers. **[secondary, Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr link above]** Not final as of the last source found.

### 2.6 Partnerships

- **[UNVERIFIED: inference from the rules above, not a sourced claim]** If Spotted Ape is under 51% black-owned, a **formal partnership or subcontract with a ≥51% (or 100%) black-owned or black-women-owned firm** lets the buyer route part of the spend through a supplier that scores on the high-weight lines. Only the spend actually invoiced by the qualifying entity counts, and **fronting** (misrepresenting ownership or control) is a criminal offence under the amended B-BBEE Act (s130). The B-BBEE Commission investigates it. Any partnership must be genuine.

## 3. Practical recommendations for Spotted Ape

1. **Get the onboarding pack ready before it's asked for:** current CoGP EME sworn affidavit (renew every 12 months, independent Commissioner of Oaths), SARS tax compliance PIN, CIPC docs, bank letter, director IDs, company profile, director CVs, and a **standard POPIA operator / data-processing addendum** plus an information-security one-pager.
2. **Register on CSD anyway.** It is free, many corporates and all SOEs/state bodies ask for a CSD report, and it opens the public-sector panels (SARB, DBSA, SABC) as a credibility and revenue path. [CSD mandatory only for organs of state, per Treasury.]
3. **Lead with the right B-BBEE message, depending on ownership (unknown):**
   - **100% black-owned:** "Level 1, 135% recognition, counts toward your EME **and** 51% black-owned targets." This is a strong selling point.
   - **51–99% black-owned:** "Level 2, 125%, counts toward EME and 51% black-owned targets."
   - **30%+ black-women-owned:** also helps the 12% women-owned line. Say so explicitly.
   - **Under 51% black-owned:** "Level 4, 100%, counts toward your EME spend target." This is useful but not differentiating. In that case consider a genuine partnership with a black-owned studio (section 2.6) and compete on expertise.
4. **Ask the buyer's procurement team** which ESD/supplier-development programme they run and whether Spotted Ape qualifies. The bank programmes (Standard Bank Tier 1/Tier 2) need you to **already be on their supplier database**, so get onboarded early, even on a small job.
5. **Land and expand:** propose a small fixed-scope project (under the client's lowest quote threshold, which you should ask them for) to get a supplier number, then pitch retainers or roster RFPs. [UNVERIFIED tactic]
6. **Watch the 2026 code amendments.** If they are finalised, 100% black-owned suppliers gain value and 51% loses some, and ESD may move to the Transformation Fund. Check the dtic site and Government Gazette.
7. **Have a B-BBEE adviser confirm** the sector template and the flow-through ownership calculation before signing the affidavit. A false affidavit is an offence.
