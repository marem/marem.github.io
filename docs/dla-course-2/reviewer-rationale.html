One case, one model, one professional decision

This 65–80 minute authentic learning activity integrates Microsoft Excel competence with professional judgement. Students interpret business data, construct an auditable model, test a material assumption, evaluate conflicting investment measures and communicate a qualified recommendation. Formative checkpoints, scaffolding and reflection move the learner from spreadsheet operator to analyst to professional adviser.

Constructive alignment

The four learner-facing outcomes connect directly to observable work:

  1. Build an auditable model: interpret inputs; enter and copy formulas; apply appropriate references and number formats. Evidence: Your Model!B4:D7, the reference-error checkpoint and optional payback ranks in row 8.
  2. Analyse financial results: compare payback and NPV, create a relevant chart and test C’s self-consumption. Evidence: chart with rationale, Test Assumptions!A7:E14 and crossover interpretation.
  3. Evaluate assumptions and claims: explain operational uncertainty and specify credible evidence before relying on the environmental claim. Evidence: load-profile explanation and Recommendation!A22.
  4. Communicate a recommendation: select a proposal using at least two correctly interpreted results, one trade-off and one operational qualification. Evidence: Recommendation!A7, limited to 100 words.

Reflection extends these outcomes to another investment decision. The detailed skills support foundational competence (understand), practical competence (model and communicate) and reflexive competence (evaluate and transfer).

Scaffolding and formative assessment

The sequence is Start → Understand → Spreadsheet Essentials → Build → Test → Decide → Reflect. Spreadsheet Essentials introduces only the prerequisite moves. Build supplies guided formulas, then asks learners to diagnose a copied-reference error. Test supplies model infrastructure so effort goes into manipulating assumptions and interpreting results, rather than transcribing a long formula.

An initial prediction makes prior judgement explicit. Answer disclosures support self-checking; diagnostic feedback and the reasoning question prompt correction. Decide’s checklist makes success criteria visible. The completed reference workbook is available on Reflect, after the modelling and recommendation tasks.

Criterion Competent performance Strong performance
Excel model Core calculations and references correct Accurate, structured and traceable to inputs
Analysis and chart Relevant comparison and sensitivity results Explains the crossover and selects a clear visual
Recommendation Quantified advice with a limitation Weighs competing measures and specifies verification needs
Scepticism and reflection Questions the claim and identifies learning Specifies credible evidence and transfers the reasoning

Professional accountancy relevance

A has the shortest payback while C has the highest base-case NPV. Neither metric automatically determines the recommendation. The learner must explain a decision rule, test the utilisation assumption and distinguish model evidence from an unverified sustainability claim.

The spreadsheet gives evidence. It does not choose the decision rule.

Microsoft Excel is the primary learning platform. Google Sheets notes provide a compatibility pathway without changing the model. The five-sheet workbook, Rand values and fictional Western Cape case maintain a coherent professional context. Tariff and escalation values are teaching assumptions, not forecasts.

This is a non-credit application demonstration. Its intended alignment is with digital, analytical, communication and professional-judgement capabilities; formal Stellenbosch module or SAICA competency mapping requires the approved outcomes and assessment documentation.
