Glossary & further reading · optional
Professional judgement
Applying relevant knowledge and weighing evidence, uncertainty and business priorities to reach a reasoned conclusion. The highest modelled NPV is one input into that judgement.
Professional scepticism
A questioning approach that critically assesses evidence and notices gaps or inconsistencies. Request support for Supplier C’s claim without assuming it is either true or dishonest.
Decision criterion
A measure or requirement used to compare alternatives, such as recovery speed, long-term value or environmental impact. Different criteria can favour different proposals.
Commercial trade-off
Accepting less of one business benefit to obtain more of another, such as slower cost recovery in exchange for higher modelled long-term value.
Operational assumption
An input about how the factory or system will operate that is used in the model but still needs evidence. C’s assumed self-consumption is material because changing it can change the ranking.
Lifecycle assessment
A structured assessment of potential environmental impacts across a product’s life, from raw materials and manufacturing through use and end-of-life treatment. A fair comparison needs a consistent method and basis of comparison.
System boundary
The stages and processes included in an environmental assessment. Excluding manufacturing or disposal can change the apparent impact, so the proposals need comparable boundaries.
Independent certification
Confirmation by a separate certifying body that specified requirements have been met. Its scope and criteria must support the particular claim being assessed; a certificate alone does not establish lower lifecycle impact.
